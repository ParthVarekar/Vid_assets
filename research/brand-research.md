### Services (website)

Ten services in three pillars:

1. **Build — Technology & Development:** websites and web applications, custom software, mobile apps, cloud and technology solutions, maintenance.
2. **Automate — AI & Automation:** AI solutions and integrations (chatbots, assistants, document processing), workflow and business automation, API and system integrations (CRMs, payment gateways, WhatsApp).
3. **Create — Design & Creative:** UI/UX and digital design, video editing and creative services.

Working model stated on the site: most projects wrap up within 2–6 weeks; revisions within agreed scope; direct access over Slack, WhatsApp, Discord or Telegram; post-launch maintenance plans. Process: Understand → Shape → Create → Elevate.

## Visual identity audit

**Colour.** The website is strictly monochrome: ink `#09090B`, white, zinc greys (`#E4E4E7`, `#9898A3`, `#6B7280`). The LinkedIn cover adds a warm drafting-paper family that the website does not show: cream `#EEEBE4`, taupe `#A69A8A`, light taupe `#DFD8CE`, with near-black `#0B0A08`. No accent colour is used consistently anywhere.

**Type.** Switzer, self-hosted on the site in Medium, Semibold and Bold. Headlines are Semibold with very tight tracking (the 76px hero uses −3.42px, about −0.045em). Labels and chips are Geist Mono.

**Logo.** A quill whose tip is a fountain-pen nib, sitting in an open cradle stroke. Black on a light ground, usually inside a soft circular badge. The largest official file I found is the 400×400 X avatar; the site ships 160–180px rasters. No vector version is published, so the film uses a vector traced from the 400px avatar (see the production plan).

**Motifs.** Soft vertical stripes behind the website hero; project screens shown as tilted cards in a row; on the LinkedIn cover, thin construction lines, a large circle arc and small squares — a drafting-table, Bauhaus-leaning geometry.

**Social content.** X and LinkedIn posts are topical tech commentary with illustrated graphics whose palettes change post to post. They do not establish a repeatable visual device.

## Inconsistencies worth knowing about

- The tagline's third word is "Scale" on LinkedIn and "grow" on the website; the website's third pillar is "Create". The film follows the brief and LinkedIn: Build. Automate. Scale.
- The name is "ProtoVinci" on the site and LinkedIn but "Proto Vinci" on X, with the handle `protovincistdio`.
- The domain is a `vercel.app` subdomain. For a B2B buyer checking credibility that reads as provisional. The film shows the URL as it currently is; a custom domain would be a simple improvement, and the end card is a single prop to change.

## What the brand communicates now, and what it should

**Now.** The website says "full-service agency, ten services, under one roof" — accurate, but it is the sentence every agency writes, and the hero line "endless solutions" is its weakest. The site's real strengths are concrete and under-used: real shipped work, short stated timelines, and practical automation examples.

**The stronger voice is on X.** "Some ideas shouldn't stay ideas" and "we build things that should already exist" are specific, confident, and tied directly to the name: *proto*type plus da *Vinci*, the artist-engineer whose notebooks are full of machines that stayed on paper. That is my reading of the name, not a statement from the brand, but it fits every asset: the quill logo, "Creativity, Engineered", and the drafting-table geometry on the LinkedIn cover.

**What it should say more clearly.** One idea: *ProtoVinci is the team that gets an idea off the page and into production.* Breadth (web, mobile, software, AI, automation) is then evidence for that idea rather than a list.

## Ideal clients (interpretation from the portfolio and copy)

- Owner-led small and mid-sized businesses moving from offline or spreadsheet operations to a real digital presence or system (catalogue, showroom, commerce).
- Institutions and operations teams with a repetitive manual process to remove (the certificate automation project is the clearest example).
- Founders who need a prototype or first product shipped in weeks without hiring in-house.

Shared trait: they have an idea or a problem they can describe, and no team to build it. The buyer is usually the decision-maker personally, watching on a phone, often with sound off.

## Positioning and message for the film

- **Positioning:** the one team that takes an idea from sketch to shipped system.
- **Single message:** some ideas shouldn't stay ideas — bring it to ProtoVinci and it gets built, automated and scaled.
- **Proof used:** the range of things built on screen, real shipped project screens, and the site's own "weeks" delivery language.
- **Call to action:** the site's own line, "Got a vision? Let's ship it.", with the URL.

Not used: client names as endorsements, testimonial quotes, any performance or growth figure, any partner or technology logo. The "Scale" scene shows multiplying units as an illustration and carries no numbers that could be read as results.

One decision for the agency to confirm: the proof scene shows screens of real client projects taken from ProtoVinci's own public portfolio. They are already public on the agency's site, but client approval for use in advertising is the agency's call. The scene is switchable with one prop (`showWork`) and falls back to abstract cards.
